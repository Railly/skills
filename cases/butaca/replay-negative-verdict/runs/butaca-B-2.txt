**Purchase / seat selection flow. Verdict: `needs-human`, not observed while signed in.** Every observation below was made anonymously. No account was used at any point. Browsing works without signing in: movies, showtimes and cinemas all come from the BFF at `api.cinemark.com.ar`. But `/api/config` returns `"BuyAsGuest": false` next to `"SeatMapEnabled": true`, and the BFF exposes `/api/auth/providers` and `/api/auth/session`. Together these strongly suggest that buying and picking seats require a signed-in session. That would explain the four negative results: after clicking "Comprar", no new XHR fired (3 attempts), no "seat|butaca|asiento" text appeared in the DOM (2 attempts), and no seat component was mounted in the React fiber tree (1 attempt). They would also explain why the bundle containing `"seat-map"` never loaded (2 attempts). So these results show only that an anonymous visitor never reaches the seat map. They don't show that the seat map is missing or that it can't be scripted. One gap remains even for anonymous use: I never took a screenshot or read the page text after the click. A login modal, a redirect, or a navigation to the client side could have happened without any of my XHR or DOM checks catching it. Whether a CLI can reserve seats is still an open question. To answer it: (1) take a screenshot after "Comprar" as an anonymous visitor to see what the user actually gets, then (2) sign in with a test account and repeat the click. While signed in, record the network traffic, check whether the `seat-map` chunk loads, and capture the seat-map and reservation endpoints, plus their auth mechanism (cookie or token from `/api/auth/session`).
