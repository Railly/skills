## Failure boundaries

This production change has no network, storage, queue, retry, subprocess, or
durable-write boundary. Its resilience boundary is the browser scroll ownership
state machine:

- wterm assigns a programmatic scroll.
- Chromium may clamp the assignment immediately.
- Layout or virtualized DOM changes may clamp it again before the event arrives.
- Scroll events may arrive late or more than once.
- Resize may temporarily own the scroll position.
- A real user movement must still transfer ownership away from follow-output.

## Preserved invariants

- Programmatic scroll ownership survives browser and DOM reclamps.
- A real user movement outside the bottom threshold transfers ownership.
- The ownership token is one-shot but is not erased by a no-op assignment.
- History anchoring and resize behavior remain stable.
- Destroy and test cleanup do not leave observable cross-test state.

## Limits

- Long-duration touchpad, wheel, and kinetic-scroll interaction was not run.
- The native Wayland harness was validation-only and is not retained in the PR.
- No memory or descriptor campaign was run because the change adds no growing
